Petition to the 125th Indiana General Assembly · 2027 session

A floor under the care, and a floor under the people who give it.

Indiana has no minimum direct care staffing standard for nursing homes. The federal one is repealed until at least 2034. This petition asks the General Assembly to pass the Indiana Nursing Home Direct Care Staffing and Accountability Act and to recognize the direct care workforce, through the Care Floor Coalition, as a party to how those homes are run.

Where $5.6 billion went

A county hospital holds the license, which unlocks a higher government-owner Medicaid rate, roughly $108 more per resident per day on a base near $297. The hospital sends the state a transfer, the state draws about two federal dollars for each local one, and the hospital keeps most of what comes back. Seven of 22 county hospitals that answered a records request admitted keeping 66 to 70 percent.

The window

How the floor disappeared, and the ten weeks that decide whether Indiana builds its own.

2027 is the long budget session, where nursing home Medicaid reimbursement is actually set. A sponsor has to be found before Organization Day or this waits until 2028, a short non-budget session.

What happened to the federal standard

  • May 2024CMS finalizes a minimum: 3.48 total hours, 0.55 RN, 2.45 aide, RN on site 24/7.
  • April 7, 2025A federal district court in Texas vacates the rule.
  • July 4, 2025Public Law 119-21 bars CMS from enforcing it until after September 30, 2034.
  • December 3, 2025CMS publishes an interim final rule repealing the hours and the 24/7 RN requirement.
  • February 2, 2026Repeal takes effect. The old rule returns: one RN on site 8 hours a day. Indiana never had a standard of its own.

  • Now to mid-November 2026The sponsor window. Bill-drafting requests and sponsor conversations. Signatures collected, county by county.

The bill

The Indiana Nursing Home Direct Care Staffing and Accountability Act

Drafted in Indiana bill format, affecting IC 16-22-15, IC 16-28-5-4, IC 16-28-9-3, and new chapters IC 16-28-17 through 16-28-20. Four parts, each written to stand alone if the bill is split.

IC 16-28-17 · Staffing and acuity verification

Sets a floor, then checks it in person.

  • 3.00 total direct care hours per resident day from July 1, 2027; 3.48 from July 1, 2028, with 2.45 CNA hours and an RN on site around the clock.
  • No shift below 80% of the daily requirement; no averaging past 24 hours; no counting hours past 16 consecutive.
  • Acuity-based staffing above the minimum, recalculated monthly.
  • Annual unannounced acuity verification: a nurse or therapist surveyor watches real transfers, weighs residents, and interviews at least five aides privately. Thirty percent of surveys start nights or weekends.
  • Per-shift public posting of census, staff scheduled, staff actually working, and the resulting hours.
  • A direct care staffing committee in every building, at least half elected floor staff.
IC 16-28-18 · Reports, investigations, systemic cause

Protects the aide who speaks.

  • Anyone may report unsafe conditions, anonymously, without telling the facility first. Reporter identity is confidential.
  • The state must acknowledge in 2 business days, investigate on site within 2, 10, or 30 days by severity, and issue written findings within 60. It may not close a report on the facility's records alone.
  • Adverse action within 180 days of a report is presumed retaliation, rebuttable only by clear and convincing evidence.
  • A private right of action: reinstatement, double back pay, at least $10,000 liquidated damages, expungement, attorney's fees. Two-year window, no exhaustion required.
  • Systemic cause determination. Before an aide is reported to the nurse aide registry, disciplined, or fired, the state must decide whether short staffing, missing equipment, a false assist level, or facility direction caused the act. If it did, the facility is cited instead.
  • Aides already on the registry may petition to expunge findings caused by those conditions, through July 1, 2030.
IC 16-28-19 · Penalties

Makes violation cost more than compliance.

  • Daily penalties of $1,000 to $10,000 per day for staffing deficits; $50,000 per person for retaliation; $100,000 for false staffing data.
  • An additional assessment equal to the money the facility saved by breaking the rule, plus 25 percent.
  • No waiving, reducing, or settling staffing and retaliation penalties. Correction does not erase the period of violation.
  • Penalties may not be paid from Medicaid money, charged to residents, or hidden in a related-party fee.
  • Owners, management companies, and landlords are jointly liable. Penalties fund a direct care restitution fund for wages, surveyors, and lifts.
IC 16-22-15 and IC 16-28-20 · The money

Requires the money to reach the care.

  • County hospitals must spend at least 80 percent of the supplemental Medicaid payments a nursing home generates on direct care at that home.
  • Not on hospital construction, debt, related-party fees, marketing, lobbying, or reserves.
  • Annual public accounting, facility by facility, audited by the State Board of Accounts; diverted amounts repaid with interest; knowing violations treated as false claims.
  • Every Medicaid nursing facility: at least 70 percent of Medicaid revenue on direct care, 55 percent on direct care wages; audited cost reports with related-party disclosure, published.
  • A direct care rate adjustment that pays more to facilities that actually staff.

The coalition

The Care Floor Coalition

An independent, Indiana-based, industry-wide coalition of the people who do direct care and the people who depend on it. Non-partisan. No dues. One argument: a resident's right only exists if someone can act on it without losing their job.

The charter: five rights on the floor

Each right is drafted into the Act. Each one is written as a resident safety measure first.

  1. The right to report, and to be believed enough to be investigated.Anonymous reports to the state, on-site investigation on a deadline, written findings, and a presumption of retaliation for 180 days afterward. For residents: a complaint line that produces a surveyor on the unit, not a phone call to the administrator.
  2. The right to refuse an unsafe assignment and write it down.Refusing a task the worker reasonably believes will endanger a resident is protected activity with a private right of action behind it. For residents: transfers done with the people and the machine the care plan says they need.
  3. The right to accurate assist levels and working equipment.A 250-pound resident, a dependent resident, a resident with two transfer falls in 90 days: two-person mechanical assist by presumption, rebuttable only by a therapist who watched. For residents: a care plan built on what is true, not what is billable or cheap.
  4. The right to a systemic cause determination before the registry.No nurse aide registry finding, discipline, or termination for an act caused by short staffing, missing equipment, a wrong assist level, or facility direction. The facility is cited instead. For residents: the cause gets fixed instead of the aide.
  5. The right to a seat where staffing is decided.A staffing committee in every building, at least half elected floor staff, with a written administrator response to every recommendation; a direct care employee on every county hospital's facility advisory committee. For residents: the people who know the hall have a vote on how it is staffed.

Who joins

  • Current CNAs, QMAs, LPNs, RNs, restorative aides, and dietary, housekeeping, laundry, and activities staff in Indiana long-term care.
  • Former staff, who can say publicly what current staff cannot.
  • Residents and their families, the group legislators actually answer.
  • Consumer, aging, disability, labor, faith, legal, and academic organizations, as endorsing members with one bounded ask each.

What it does between now and January

  • Collects and verifies petition signatures, county by county, and delivers them through each county's own legislators.
  • Runs know-your-rights education: NLRA §7 concerted activity, the 30-day IOSHA retaliation window, how to file a complaint the Ombudsman can verify.
  • Keeps a quiet county-contact network so a worker in trouble has one person nearby who knows the process.
  • Builds a testimony bench of former staff and families for the committee hearing.
  • Sends and tracks the fifteen public records requests in the Indiana Nursing Home Ledger.

What it is not

  • Not a union and not a substitute for one. Members may organize under federal labor law; the coalition does not bargain.
  • Not legal advice, and not a place to send resident information, photos from inside a building, or work documents. Ever.
  • Not partisan. The votes this needs are in both caucuses.
30 days

If something happens to you after you report a safety problem, the window to file a retaliation complaint with the Indiana Department of Labor is 30 calendar days from the adverse action. Most people lose it appealing internally. Write everything down, with dates, on your own phone.

Indiana DOL Whistleblower Protection Unit; federal OSHA §11(c) is also 30 days. General information, not legal advice.

Petition to the Indiana General Assembly
For a state direct care staffing floor, protection of the caregivers who report, and accountability for nursing home Medicaid dollars

To the Honorable Speaker and Members of the House of Representatives, and the Honorable President Pro Tempore and Members of the Senate, of the 125th General Assembly of the State of Indiana:

The undersigned, residents of the State of Indiana, exercising the right secured by Article 1, Section 31 of the Constitution of the State of Indiana to instruct our representatives and to apply to the General Assembly for redress of grievances, respectfully represent:

WHEREAS, Indiana law sets no minimum number of certified nurse aide hours or total direct care hours per resident day in comprehensive care facilities, requiring only one-half hour of licensed nurse time per resident day under 410 IAC 16.2-3.1-17, and Indiana is one of eighteen states with no direct care staffing minimum of any kind;

WHEREAS, an analysis of federal payroll-based staffing data published March 18, 2025 ranked Indiana fiftieth of the fifty states and the District of Columbia for total nurse staffing hours per resident after adjusting for resident acuity, and federal data for the first quarter of 2026 show that eighty-eight percent of Indiana nursing homes staffed below their residents' assessed needs;

WHEREAS, the federal minimum staffing standard finalized in May 2024 was repealed effective February 2, 2026, and Public Law 119-21 bars its enforcement until after September 30, 2034, so that no federal floor will exist for the remainder of this decade unless the State of Indiana sets one;

WHEREAS, county-owned hospitals hold the licenses to most Indiana nursing homes and, according to published investigative reporting drawing on records obtained through litigation, retained roughly $2.6 billion of $5.6 billion in supplemental Medicaid payments generated by those homes over approximately fifteen years, applying it to hospital construction, equipment, and operations rather than bedside care;

WHEREAS, a resident's rights are exercised through the frontline caregiver, and Indiana's at-will employment law, together with a thirty-day window to file a retaliation complaint, leaves that caregiver exposed to discipline, termination, and nurse aide registry referral for acts and omissions caused by conditions the facility created; and

WHEREAS, the safety of residents and the safety of the workers who care for them are one problem, and no resident right can be enforced by a worker who cannot act on it without losing their livelihood;

NOW, THEREFORE, your petitioners respectfully pray that the General Assembly:
  1. Introduce, hear in committee, and enact in the 2027 regular session the Indiana Nursing Home Direct Care Staffing and Accountability Act, adding IC 16-28-17, IC 16-28-18, IC 16-28-19, IC 16-28-20, and IC 16-22-15 to the Indiana Code and amending IC 16-28-5-4 and IC 16-28-9-3;
  2. Establish thereby a minimum direct care staffing standard of 3.00 total hours per resident day beginning July 1, 2027 and 3.48 hours beginning July 1, 2028, including 2.45 certified nurse aide hours and a registered nurse on site twenty-four hours a day, with acuity-based staffing above the minimum, annual unannounced acuity verification surveys, and public posting of staffing on every shift;
  3. Protect every direct care employee who reports an unsafe condition or refuses an unsafe assignment, through a rebuttable presumption of retaliation, a private right of action, and a systemic cause determination before any nurse aide registry finding, discipline, or termination for an act or omission caused by staffing below the standard, missing or inoperable equipment, an inaccurate assist level, or facility direction;
  4. Provide that penalties for staffing and retaliation violations exceed the cost of compliance, are not reduced upon correction, are not paid from Medicaid funds or charged to residents, and are deposited in a direct care restitution fund;
  5. Require county hospitals that hold nursing facility licenses to spend not less than eighty percent of the nursing facility supplemental Medicaid payments attributable to a facility on direct care at that facility, to file annual public facility-level accountings, and to repay diverted amounts; and
  6. Recognize the direct care workforce as a party to nursing home policy in Indiana, by consulting the Care Floor Coalition and the facility direct care staffing committees created by the Act in the development of rules, reimbursement, and oversight affecting long-term care, and by inviting direct care workers, residents, and families to testify at any hearing on the Act.

Respectfully submitted by the undersigned residents of Indiana, each of whom has signed this petition once, in their own hand, on the date shown.

How a petition works at the Statehouse

      

        
What a petition can do in Indiana, and what it cannot.

        
Indiana has no citizen initiative or referendum. A petition cannot put a law on the ballot or force a vote. Its power is that a legislator presents it, it enters the record, it is referred to a committee, and every office that receives it counts the names from its own district.

      

    

    

      

        
The right

        
"No law shall restrain any of the inhabitants of the State from assembling together in a peaceable manner, to consult for their common good; nor from instructing their representatives; nor from applying to the General Assembly for redress of grievances."

        Constitution of the State of Indiana, Article 1, Section 31. This petition is written as an instruction to representatives and an application for redress under that section.
      

      

        
How it enters the chamber

        
    
          
  • A member of the House or Senate presents a petition or memorial. It is filed with that chamber's clerk (the Principal Clerk of the House, the Principal Secretary of the Senate) and referred by the presiding officer to a committee.
    • 
          
  • Senate standing rules require a petition or memorial to be reported on by the committee it is assigned to before any action is taken on it.
    • 
          
  • The likely committees for this subject are House Public Health and Senate Health and Provider Services.
    • 
        

        Standing Rules and Orders of the Senate; Rules of the House of Representatives, published each term at iga.in.gov. Confirm the current rule numbers with the Principal Clerk and Principal Secretary before delivery.
      

      

        
The form each sheet follows

        
    
          
  • Identical petition text on every sheet, with sheet numbers and the circulator's name.
    • 
          
  • Each signer's signature, printed name, and residence address in the signer's own handwriting, the convention Indiana's statutory petitions require under IC 3-8-6-6, plus county and date on each line.
    • 
          
  • Indiana residents only, one signature per person, no notarization required.
    • 
          
  • Online signatures are printed as a schedule attached to the paper petition, with a statement of how and when they were collected.
    • 
        

        IC 3-8-6-6 governs candidate petitions, not legislative ones; its signer conventions are used here because they are what Indiana clerks are used to reading.
      

    

    

      Two things to keep straight. Ask family members and former staff to be the public face of delivery; a current employee's name on a cover letter is a risk the coalition should not ask anyone to take. And the petition is not a lawsuit or a records request: it asks for a law, it does not describe any facility, and it names no one as a wrongdoer.
    

  


  
  

    

      
Deliver it

      

        
Ninety-two counties, one hundred fifty legislators, one ask each.

        
A thousand signatures in one box at the Statehouse is a photo. Ten signatures from a legislator's own district, handed to them by a constituent, is a conversation.
