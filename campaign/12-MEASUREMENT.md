# Measurement

## The only metrics that matter

Follower count is vanity for this campaign. A page with 800 followers and 25 workers
willing to talk beats a page with 20,000 followers and none. Track these instead:

**Tier 1 — the campaign is working**
1. Confidential contacts received (workers, families, separately)
2. County contacts recruited and retained
3. Former workers willing to go on record
4. Legislator contacts generated
5. Reporter conversations
6. Email list size

**Tier 2 — the content is working**
7. Saves and shares (not likes — saves mean useful, shares mean it traveled)
8. Reach on the receipts posts specifically
9. Group post reply counts
10. Link clicks to the rights and complaint pages

**Tier 3 — background**
11. Followers, likes, comments

If Tier 2 is climbing and Tier 1 is flat, the content is good and the **conversion path is
broken** — check that the contact link is visible, that the safety notice isn't scaring
people off, and that you're actually inviting contact rather than only informing.

## Weekly review (15 minutes, Sunday)

- Top two posts by saves. Why did those work?
- Bottom two posts. Kill that format or fix it.
- Contacts this week, and where each came from
- Anything that needs a fact re-verified
- Next week scheduled?

## Monthly review (1 hour)

- Tier 1 numbers vs. the 90-day targets in `00-STRATEGY.md`
- Which platform produced the most Tier 1 outcomes per hour spent? **Cut the worst one.**
  You cannot sustain five platforms alone, and the calendar assumes you'll eventually drop
  to three.
- Re-check every statistic older than six months
- Recheck published phone numbers and URLs
- Is the litigation posture in `09-LEGAL-SAFETY.md` still current?

## Contact log

Keep offline, not in this repo, not in any cloud doc tied to your name. Fields:

`date · first name or nickname · role · county · shift · how they found you · what they
need · permission to publish (Y/N, in writing) · follow-up date · county contact (Y/N)`

**Never commit this file to git.** A `.gitignore` entry is included.

## Realistic expectations

- **Weeks 1–4:** almost nothing. Under 100 followers. Maybe 2–3 contacts. This is normal
  and is not a signal to change strategy.
- **Weeks 5–8:** one post outperforms the rest by 10–50×. It will probably be "The Math"
  or "30 days." When it happens, immediately post two follow-ups in the same format
  while the audience is there.
- **Months 3–6:** the account becomes a known quantity among Indiana CNAs. Contacts arrive
  without prompting. A reporter calls.
- **The failure mode is not low numbers. It's you stopping.** Consistency at low volume
  beats intensity followed by silence, every time.

## Kill criteria

Stop doing something if, after 8 weeks:
- A platform has produced zero Tier 1 outcomes → drop it
- A content series gets no saves → replace it
- Group posting gets you removed from groups → change approach, not volume

## Sustainability

You are the whole campaign right now. Protect the one asset it depends on.

- Batch on Sunday. Never write reactively.
- One day a week fully off the accounts.
- Do not read every comment on a video that breaks out. Set a timer.
- Recruit a second admin by month three.
- When a stranger's story is genuinely upsetting, respond, log it, and close the tab.

The thing this campaign is about — people being ground down until they quit or break — is
a real risk to the person running it. Build the habits now, while it's small.
