## Medicaid diversion / county hospital ownership

**[V]** County hospitals acquired nearly all of Indiana's ~500 nursing homes over roughly
15 years. Twenty-one public hospitals retained about **46% of $5.6 billion** generated by
those homes — **more than $2.6 billion** — spent on hospital buildings, renovations,
equipment, ambulances, and technology.

**[V]** Government ownership qualifies a home for a Medicaid rate about **36% higher**
than a privately owned facility. Reported as an additional **$108.30 per patient per day**
on top of a standard rate of **$297.42**. *(Rates change — date-qualify or omit the dollar
figures.)*

**[V]** In Indianapolis, the Health & Hospital Corporation of Marion County drew on funds
generated by **more than 70 nursing homes** toward the **$754 million** Sidney & Lois
Eskenazi Hospital.

**[V]** IndyStar filed a public records lawsuit in **2022** against **eight** county
hospitals that refused to disclose how the money was spent.

**Attribution — get this right.** "Careless" is the **2020** IndyStar investigation, which
documented over $1 billion. The **$2.6 billion** figure comes from Tony Cook's continuation
of that reporting with Tim Evans and Emily Hopkins, published **March 2025**. Attribute as
*"IndyStar's investigation, 2020 'Careless' series continued March 2025."* Calling the
$2.6B figure "Careless" is imprecise.

## Staffing

**[V]** Indiana ranks **50th out of 50 states and D.C.** for total nurse staffing hours per
resident, adjusted for resident acuity — down from an already poor 2020 ranking. IndyStar
analysis of federal payroll-based journal (PBJ) data, March 2025.

**[V]** Acuity-adjusted, Indiana provides **3.51 total nurse staffing hours per resident
day** against a national average of **3.76**, falling short across RN, LPN, and aide.

**[V]** Indiana staffs **29.6% below its residents' expected need** and ranks among the
**ten worst states** for overall staffing. Q1 2026 federal data.

**[V]** Nationally, **90.2%** of nursing homes report staffing below expected levels. The
median home falls **24.9%** short on total nurse staffing and **42.3%** short on RN
staffing. US average: **3.75 total HPRD**, **0.61 RN HPRD** (Q1 2026, down from 3.76 in
Q4 2025).

- https://nursinghome411.org/alert-staffing-q1-2026/

## Federal minimum staffing rule — repealed

**[V]** CMS final rule, May 2024: **3.48** total HPRD, **0.55** RN, **2.45** nurse aide,
plus an RN onsite **24/7**. Codified at 42 C.F.R. §§ 483.5 and 483.35.

**[V]** **Vacated April 7, 2025**, U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Texas
(Judge Kacsmaryk). The administration voluntarily dismissed its appeal in October 2025.

**[V]** **Section 71111 of Public Law 119-21** (signed **July 4, 2025**) bars CMS from
implementing, administering, or enforcing those standards **until after September 30,
2034**.

**[V]** CMS interim final rule repealing the standards published **December 3, 2025**,
**effective February 2, 2026**.

## Indiana law and legislature

**[V]** The 2026 short session **adjourned sine die February 27, 2026**.

**[V]** The **2027 session is the long budget session**, convening the **first full week of
January 2027** and running through **late April**.

**[V]** **HEA 1277** (signed by Gov. Braun, 2026): beginning **July 1, 2027**, a Medicaid
recipient who has received nursing facility services for **100 consecutive days** leaves
managed care (PathWays) and receives services through fee-for-service. Indiana is the
first managed-LTSS state to publicly reverse course on its long-stay nursing home
population. Driven by a finding that PathWays cost **$91 million per year more** than
fee-for-service. Sets waiver cost limits and directs savings toward a waitlist of nearly
**12,000** people. Requires FSSA to apply by **September 1, 2026** for a separate assisted
living waiver for people 60+ at nursing-facility level of care.

via intergovernmental transfers rather than paying the assessment directly.

## Retaliation and worker rights

**[V]** **IOSHA retaliation: 30 calendar days.** Statutory language — an employee who
believes they were discharged or retaliated against for protected activity may, "within 30
calendar days after the alleged offense," file a complaint with the Indiana Department of
Labor.

**[V]** To prevail, a complainant must show they engaged in protected activity, the
employer knew about it, the employer took adverse action, and the protected activity was
the motivating factor. Protected activity includes reporting hazards to management and
reporting a work-related injury or illness. Retaliation includes firing, layoff, demotion,
and denial of overtime or promotion.

**[V]** Federal OSHA § 11(c) carries the same **30-day** window.

**[V]** Indiana is an **at-will** employment state.

**[V1]** NLRA § 7 protects most private-sector employees engaged in concerted activity over
wages, hours, and working conditions, regardless of union status. Supervisors are generally
excluded. *(Well-established law; stated generally in all campaign materials, never applied
to an individual's situation.)*
