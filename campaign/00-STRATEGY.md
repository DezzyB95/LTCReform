# Together Network — Social Media Campaign Strategy

Indiana long-term care reform. Version 1, built for a project with no following yet.

---

## 1. The one-sentence case

Indiana pays more per nursing home resident than most states and staffs them worse than
every other state — because the money is legally allowed to leave the building.

That sentence is the campaign. Everything else supports it.

## 2. Why this framing wins

Most nursing home advocacy leads with sympathy ("our seniors deserve better"). That
message has existed in Indiana for 20 years and Indiana still ranks 50th. It fails
because it doesn't name a mechanism or a decision-maker — it just asks people to feel bad.

This campaign leads with **a documented money trail** and **a documented outcome**. It
converts a feelings argument into an accounting argument. Accounting arguments get
reporters, legislators, and family members who were previously neutral.

The second pillar is the one nobody else is running: **worker safety and resident safety
are the same problem.** Resident-rights education can't work when the person responsible
for upholding those rights is fired for upholding them. That's a genuinely new frame in
Indiana LTC advocacy and it's the reason this project exists rather than duplicating
AARP or the Ombudsman.

## 3. Goals — first 90 days

This is a cold start. Do not set follower goals. Set contact goals.

| Goal | Target by Day 90 | How measured |
|---|---|---|
| Confidential worker contacts | 25 | Form submissions + DMs |
| Workers who agree to be a "county contact" | 5 | Named list, private |
| Family/resident contacts | 15 | Form submissions |
| Legislator contacts generated | 100 | Action-tool clicks / self-reported |
| One earned media hit | 1 | Reporter published or quoted |
| Email list | 200 | List size |

Followers are a lagging indicator of the above. Ignore them for 90 days.

## 4. Audiences, in priority order

**1. Current Indiana LTC staff (CNAs, QMAs, LPNs, RNs, dietary, housekeeping, laundry).**
The core. They are afraid, exhausted, and scrolling at 11pm after a double. They will
not comment publicly. They will read, save, and DM. Design every post assuming the best
outcome is a silent save and a private message — never a public comment.

**2. Former LTC staff.** No retaliation risk anymore. These are your public voices, your
comment-section defenders, and your on-record quotes for reporters. Recruit them
deliberately.

**3. Families of residents.** Highest emotional engagement, highest share rate, and they
can speak publicly without losing a job. They're also the group legislators actually
answer. Families are your amplification engine.

**4. Reporters, legislators, and allied orgs.** Small in number, disproportionate in
effect. They are watching quietly long before they engage. Post as if a reporter reads
every post, because eventually one will.

**5. General public.** Lowest priority. Do not write for them. Writing for them is what
produces the vague inspirational content that has never changed anything in this state.

## 5. Voice

Plain, calm, specific, unhurried. A coworker talking to coworkers on break.

- Short sentences. Concrete nouns. Real numbers.
- No exclamation points. No "heartbreaking." No "our most vulnerable." No emoji strings.
- No begging, no guilt, no outrage-bait, no all-caps.
- Never inspirational-poster tone. Never nonprofit-grant tone. Never law-firm tone.
- Say the number, say the source, say what to do. Then stop.

The tone test: **would a 15-year CNA roll their eyes at this?** If yes, rewrite it.

## 6. Platforms

| Platform | Role | Cadence |
|---|---|---|
| **Facebook (Page + targeted Group posting)** | Primary. Indiana LTC workers and families live here, especially 35+. County-level and CNA groups are where recruitment actually happens. | 4–5×/week |
| **TikTok** | Reach engine. Young CNAs. Text-over-b-roll works; no face needed. Highest odds of a breakout. | 3–4×/week |
| **Instagram (Reels + carousels)** | Repost TikToks, plus saveable carousels. Carousels are the "rights card" format. | 3–4×/week |
| **Bluesky / X** | Reporters, legislators, policy people. Low volume, high signal. Post the receipts here. | 3×/week |
| **LinkedIn** | Administrators, DONs, nurse leaders, agency staff, WGU network. Different register — professional, data-forward. | 1×/week |
| **Reddit (r/CNA, r/nursing, r/Indiana, r/Indianapolis)** | Recruitment, not broadcast. Participate as a person; do not spam links. | As relevant |

Skip YouTube long-form and a podcast for now. Both cost more time than they return at
this stage.

## 7. Content mix

Run a repeating weekly pattern rather than inventing content each day.

- **30% Receipts** — the money, the rankings, the rule repeal. Sourced, screenshot-able.
- **25% Know Your Rights** — one right, one card, one plain explanation.
- **20% Reality of the floor** — the math of the shift. Time, ratios, bodies.
- **15% Action** — call this person, file this complaint, show up here.
- **10% Project/community** — what Together Network is, what came in this week (anonymized).

## 8. The 5 recurring series (build these, don't freestyle)

1. **"The Math"** — one shift, one number. *20 residents. 8 hours. 480 minutes. Minus
   charting, minus meals, minus report. That's under 20 minutes per person, per shift,
   for everything.* Runs weekly. This is the flagship series.
2. **"Where the Money Went"** — the IndyStar findings, one piece at a time. County
   hospitals, the 36% rate bump, the buildings it bought. Runs weekly.
3. **"You're Allowed To"** — one worker or resident right per card. *You're allowed to
   talk to coworkers about staffing. You're allowed to refuse an unsafe assignment and
   document it. You're allowed to file anonymously.* Runs weekly.
4. **"They Told Us"** — verbatim things management says, without commentary. *"Move
   faster." "Do more with less." "You're a servant."* Devastating precisely because it's
   flat. Runs every other week.
5. **"Ask a CNA"** — families submit questions, a CNA answers plainly. Builds the
   family–worker alliance that this whole campaign depends on. Runs every other week.

## 9. What this campaign will not do

- Not name a specific facility or employer as a wrongdoer. (See `09-LEGAL-SAFETY.md` —
  this is a hard rule, not a preference.)
- Not post resident information, photos, room details, or anything PHI-adjacent, ever.
- Not tell workers to quit. Tell them how to stay and document.
- Not run a public forum or comment-based support group. Support happens privately.
- Not fundraise in the first 90 days. Asking for money before you've delivered anything
  is how new projects lose credibility permanently.
- Not become a partisan account. The mechanism is bipartisan-fixable and framing it as a
  Democratic issue cuts the audience in half in Indiana.

## 10. The 90-day arc

- **Weeks 1–2 — Establish credibility.** Receipts only. No asks. Prove you're serious
  and sourced before you request anything from anyone.
- **Weeks 3–4 — Establish usefulness.** Rights cards, documentation templates, how to
  file. Become the account people save.
- **Weeks 5–8 — Establish belonging.** "You're not the only one." Recruit county
  contacts. Open confidential intake wide.
- **Weeks 9–12 — Establish power.** Point everything at the 2027 long session and the
  interim study committees. Convert audience into legislator contacts.

## 11. The single most important strategic point

The 2026 short session ended February 27, 2026. The 2027 session is the **long budget
session** — the one where Medicaid reimbursement and LTC funding are actually decided,
and where HEA 1277's shift of long-stay residents back to fee-for-service (effective
July 1, 2027) will be reopened and argued.

Everything between now and January 2027 is the organizing window. The interim study
committee period is when legislators are cheap to reach and nobody is competing for
their attention. **Build the list now so you can move it in January.**
