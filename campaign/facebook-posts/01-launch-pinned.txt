Indiana nursing homes rank 50th in the country for staffing.

Not in the bottom ten. Fiftieth — last, out of all fifty states and D.C. — for total nurse staffing hours per resident, once you account for how sick the residents are.

That is not because the money isn't there.

Over about fifteen years, Indiana nursing homes generated roughly $5.6 billion in Medicaid payments through county hospital ownership. Twenty-one county hospitals kept about 46% of it — more than $2.6 billion — and spent it on hospital buildings, equipment, and ambulances.

The money came in for nursing home care. It left before it got to the floor.

I'm a CNA. I've worked in Indiana long-term care for over ten years.

Together Network is a project for the people who are still in the building — CNAs, QMAs, nurses, dietary, housekeeping, laundry — and for the residents and families depending on them.
