Indiana does not require a nursing home to give a resident a single hour of
aide care per day.

Not a low number. No number. State law sets half an hour of licensed nurse
time per resident per day and nothing at all for certified nurse aides.
Indiana is one of eighteen states with no direct care minimum of any kind.

There used to be a federal one. It was finalized in 2024, struck down in
court in 2025, and Congress barred anyone from enforcing a replacement until
after September 30, 2034. The repeal took effect this February.

So for the rest of the decade, if Indiana does not set a floor, nobody will.

There is a petition to the General Assembly asking for three things. A state
minimum staffing standard. Protection for the caregivers who report unsafe
conditions, because a resident's right only exists if someone can act on it
without losing their job. And a requirement that the Medicaid money these
homes generate is spent on care inside them.

Indiana residents can sign it. Link in the first comment, along with the
sources for every number above.

LINKEDIN — image 09-wide, or images 1, 5 and 6
================================================================

Indiana sets no minimum number of direct care hours a nursing home resident
must receive. State law requires half an hour of licensed nurse time per
resident per day and no certified nurse aide time at all. Indiana is one of
eighteen states with no direct care staffing minimum.

The federal standard that would have applied was finalized in May 2024,
vacated by a federal court in April 2025, and barred from enforcement by
Public Law 119-21 until after September 30, 2034. The repeal took effect
February 2, 2026.

The 2027 session is a budget session, which is where nursing facility
Medicaid reimbursement is decided. A petition to the 125th General Assembly
asks for a phased staffing standard reaching 3.48 hours per resident day,
anti-retaliation protection for direct care staff who report unsafe
conditions, and a requirement that county hospitals holding nursing facility
licenses spend at least 80 percent of the supplemental Medicaid payments a
facility generates on direct care at that facility.

The full text, the draft bill, and the sources for every figure are at
https://staffthefloor.netlify.app
