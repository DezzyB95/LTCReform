# TikTok / Reels / Shorts — Scripts

Your reach engine. Young CNAs are here and they are the least reached and most burned-out
segment in Indiana LTC.

## Production rules

- **No face required.** Text on screen over hands, a parking lot at 5:40am, scrubs in a
  dryer, a car dashboard, a whiteboard, a notebook. Faceless performs fine and protects
  everyone.
- **Never film inside a facility.** Not the parking lot with a sign visible, not a badge,
  not scrubs with a logo. This is non-negotiable.
- **Hook in the first 1.5 seconds**, on screen as text, not spoken. Most people watch
  muted.
- 25–45 seconds. Under 20 doesn't hold the argument; over 60 loses the audience.
- Captions burned in, always.
- Flat delivery. Do not perform outrage. The numbers do the work.
- Post 3–4×/week. Repost the winners to Reels and Shorts the next day.

## Script 1 — "Fiftieth" (launch video)

**Hook text:** `Indiana is 50th out of 50 for nursing home staffing.`

**Visual:** plain text on solid background, one line at a time.

> Indiana ranks last in the country for nursing home staffing hours per resident.
>
> Not bottom five. Last.
>
> Now here's the part that doesn't fit.
>
> Over about fifteen years, Indiana nursing homes generated five point six billion dollars
> in Medicaid money through county hospital ownership.
>
> Twenty-one county hospitals kept about forty-six percent of it. Two point six billion
> dollars.
>
> It went to hospital buildings. Equipment. Ambulances.
>
> The money came in for nursing home care. It never reached the floor.
>
> That's not a shortage. That's a decision.

**Caption:** Source: IndyStar "Careless" investigation. Indiana is 50th in the nation for
staffing. #cna #nursinghome #indiana #ltc

## Script 2 — "The Math" (highest replay potential)

**Hook text:** `20 residents. 8 hours. Let's do the math.`

**Visual:** hand writing the subtraction on paper, or numbers appearing on screen.

> Twenty residents. Eight hours. That's 480 minutes.
>
> Minus report. Minus charting. Minus two meal passes. Minus fifteen minutes looking for
> the one working lift.
>
> Under twenty minutes per person. For the whole shift.
>
> In twenty minutes you're supposed to do two rounds of care, reposition, shower if it's
> their day, get them up, feed them if they need it, answer the light, and be a person to
> someone whose family hasn't visited in a month.
>
> Then they tell you to move faster.
>
> Nobody is failing at this job. The job doesn't fit inside the time.

## Script 3 — "Two-person assist"

**Hook text:** `The care plan says two people. There's one of you.`

> Every care plan in the building says two-person assist.
>
> There is one person on the floor.
>
> So you do it alone and risk your back and their hip. Or you don't, and they sit in it
> until somebody comes.
>
> Those are the two options. The third option — the second person — got removed on a
> spreadsheet.
>
> And when somebody gets hurt, the incident report asks what you did wrong.

## Script 4 — "30 days" (highest save rate; repost quarterly)

**Hook text:** `You have 30 days. Most people don't know this.`

> If you get fired, demoted, or written up after you report a safety problem in Indiana —
> you have thirty calendar days to file a retaliation complaint with the Indiana
> Department of Labor.
>
> Thirty calendar days. Not thirty business days. Not thirty days after HR gets back to
> you.
>
> Most people lose that window because they spend the first month going through internal
> appeals. The clock keeps running.
>
> File first. Appeal internally at the same time if you want to.
>
> Indiana DOL, IOSHA whistleblower unit. General information, not legal advice.

## Script 5 — "You're allowed to"

**Hook text:** `Things they told you that you can't do. You can.`

> You're allowed to talk to your coworkers about staffing.
>
> You're allowed to talk about your pay.
>
> You're allowed to talk about scheduling and safety.
>
> You're allowed to do it together, as a group. Federal labor law protects most
> private-sector workers acting together over working conditions, union or no union.
>
> Protected doesn't mean nobody will retaliate. It means retaliation is illegal and you
> can file on it.
>
> Which is why you write everything down. Dates, times, names, on your own phone, off the
> clock.

---

## Script 6 — "The floor is gone"

**Hook text:** `There is no minimum staffing law right now. None.`

> In 2024 the federal government finally set a minimum staffing standard for nursing
> homes. Three point four eight nursing hours per resident per day. An RN in the building
> twenty-four seven.
>
> April 2025, a federal court struck it down.
>
> Then Congress blocked it from being enforced until after 2034.
>
> February 2026, it was formally repealed.
>
> Indiana never had a state minimum of its own.
>
> So right now, today, there is no minimum staffing requirement in an Indiana nursing
> home. There won't be a federal one for at least eight years.
>
> Whatever the state does is the only thing that exists now.

## Script 7 — "They told us"

**Hook text:** `Real things management says. No commentary.`

**Visual:** text only, plain, on black. Long pauses. Silence or a very quiet bed.

>
> "Do more with less."
>
> "Everybody gets out of bed. I don't care what they want."
>
> "You're a servant."
>
> That's the job.

## Script 8 — "The write-up"

**Hook text:** `She said no. The aide got written up.`

> A resident has a legal right to refuse care. It's in federal regulation.
>
> A resident says she doesn't want to get out of bed today.
>
> The aide honors it, because that's the law and because she's a person, not a schedule.
>
> The aide gets written up for it.
>
> This is why resident-rights education by itself does nothing. The rights are real. The
> person responsible for honoring them gets punished for honoring them.
>
> Fix that, and the rights start working on their own.

---

## Script 9 — For families

**Hook text:** `The aide taking care of your mom is on your side.`

> If you have someone in a nursing home, here's something worth knowing.
>
> The aide can see everything you see. She probably can't say it out loud.
>
> So ask her supervisor instead, in writing. How many aides are on this hall today. What
> was it overnight. What was it Saturday. How many are agency staff who've never met my
> mother.
>
> Ask by email so there's a record.
>
> You can say the thing she'd be fired for saying. That's the most useful thing you have.

## Script 10 — "Why this exists"

**Hook text:** `Why I started this.`

**Visual:** parking lot at dawn, or hands, no face.

> I've been a CNA in Indiana for over ten years.
>
> I tried the state. I tried IOSHA. I tried the ombudsman. I tried CMS. I filed the
> complaints you're supposed to file.
>
> The conditions didn't change.
>
> What I figured out is that resident advocacy and worker advocacy in Indiana are two
> separate things, and that's exactly why neither one works. You cannot ask someone to
> protect a resident's rights when protecting those rights costs them their job.
>
> So this is a project for both at once. Workers, residents, families.
>
> No fundraising. No naming facilities. Just what's documented, and what you're allowed
> to do about it.

*Keep it exactly this general. See `09-LEGAL-SAFETY.md`.*

## Comment strategy

Comments are where video accounts are actually built.

- Answer every question in the first two hours. Reach follows early comment velocity.
- When someone says "this is my building too" → reply publicly with something short and
  human, then let them come to you. Never DM first about their workplace.
- When someone attacks: answer once, calmly, with a source. Never twice.
- Pin the best worker comment on every video. It does more than anything you write.
