# Message Platform & Verified Proof Points

Every number below has a source. **Do not post a statistic that isn't on this page**, and
re-check anything older than six months before reusing it. Credibility is the only asset
a new project has.

---

## Core message

> Indiana sends more money per nursing home resident than most states, and staffs those
> homes worse than any other state. The money is real. The staffing is real. The gap
> between them is a policy choice, and it can be changed.

## Three supporting pillars

### Pillar 1 — The money left the building

Indiana county-owned hospitals acquired nearly all of the state's roughly 500 nursing
homes over about 15 years, which qualified those homes for a substantially higher
Medicaid reimbursement rate. The IndyStar's multi-year "Careless" investigation found
that 21 public hospitals kept roughly 46% of the $5.6 billion those homes generated —
more than $2.6 billion diverted away from nursing home care and spent on hospital
buildings, renovations, equipment, ambulances, and technology.

- Source: IndyStar's investigation by Tony Cook, Tim Evans and Emily Hopkins, published
  March 2025 as a continuation of their 2020 "Careless" series. Attribute it that way —
  "Careless" alone refers to the 2020 reporting, which documented over $1 billion.
  Summarized by
  [Becker's Hospital Review](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/post-acute/indiana-hospitals-divert-2-6b-in-medicaid-from-nursing-homes-report/),
  [Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/2-6-billion-medicaid-funds-diverted-indiana-care-homes-report-2046425),
  and [Journalist's Resource](https://journalistsresource.org/health/nursing-homes-dollars-diverted-county-hospitals-indiana/).
- IndyStar had to sue eight county hospitals in 2022 to get the financial records at all.
  That detail is worth posting on its own — it tells people the opacity is deliberate.

**Say it as:** "Indiana nursing homes got the money. Indiana nursing homes didn't get to
keep it."

### Pillar 2 — Indiana staffs worse than any other state

- Indiana ranks **50th out of 50 states and D.C.** for total nurse staffing hours per
  resident after adjusting for how sick residents are — a drop from an already poor 2020
  ranking. (IndyStar analysis of federal payroll-based staffing data.)
- Acuity-adjusted, Indiana provides **3.51 total nurse staffing hours per resident day
  against a national average of 3.76** — short in all three roles (RN, LPN, aide).
  (IndyStar analysis of federal payroll-based journal data, March 2025.)
- Indiana staffs **29.6% below its own residents' expected need** and ranks among the ten
  worst states for overall staffing. Nationally, **90.2% of nursing homes** report
  staffing below expected levels; the median home falls 24.9% short on total nurse
  staffing and 42.3% short on RN staffing. Source: [Long Term Care Community Coalition,
  Q1 2026 staffing alert](https://nursinghome411.org/alert-staffing-q1-2026/).

**Do not cite Indiana's RN hours per resident day on its own.** Indiana sits near the
national RN average, so that single number reads as unremarkable and invites a rebuttal
that misses the point. Indiana's deficit is in *total* staffing and in staffing measured
against resident need — use the acuity-adjusted figures above instead.

**Do not cite the AARP LTSS scorecard.** Its most recent edition (2023) ranks Indiana
**27th overall**, up from 41st in 2020, and ranks Indiana **1st in the nation** for
age-friendly care. An older, worse AARP number is out of date, and citing it hands a
critic an easy correction.

**Say it as:** "Fiftieth. Not in the bottom ten. Fiftieth."

### Pillar 3 — There is no longer a federal floor, and there is no state one

- CMS finalized minimum staffing standards in May 2024: 3.48 total nurse hours per
  resident day, 0.55 RN, 2.45 nurse aide, plus an RN onsite 24/7.
- A federal judge in the Northern District of Texas **vacated** that rule on April 7,
  2025. Source: [Modern Healthcare](https://www.modernhealthcare.com/providers/nursing-home-staffing-mandate-struck-down/), [McKnight's](https://www.mcknights.com/news/breaking-judge-tosses-nursing-home-staffing-rule/).
- **Section 71111 of Public Law 119-21 bars CMS from implementing or enforcing those
  standards until after September 30, 2034.** Source: [Fisher Phillips](https://www.fisherphillips.com/en/insights/insights/minimum-staffing-rules-for-long-term-care-facilities-tossed-out-by-federal-courts-and-budget-bill).
- CMS then issued an interim final rule (published December 3, 2025, effective February 2,
  2026) formally repealing the standards. Source: [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/12/03/2025-21792/medicare-and-medicaid-programs-repeal-of-minimum-staffing-standards-for-long-term-care-facilities),
  [Consumer Voice](https://theconsumervoice.org/news/cms-takes-action-to-rescind-minimum-staffing-rule/).

**Say it as:** "The federal minimum staffing standard is gone until at least 2034. Indiana
never had one of its own. Right now there is no floor."

This is the most under-communicated fact in Indiana LTC and it is the strongest argument
for **state** action. Federal rescue is off the table for a decade. That's the whole case
for a state staffing standard.

## The worker-safety bridge (the argument nobody else is making)

The standard Indiana response to LTC complaints is resident-rights education. The
Ombudsman's office says it plainly: resident councils, grievance policies, detailed
complaints, and — on staff treatment — "we have no control over how the facility
treats their staff."

That's the gap this project fills. State the bridge like this:

> A resident's right only exists if someone can act on it without losing their job.
> When a resident refuses care and the aide honors that refusal, the aide gets written
> up. So the right is on paper and the retaliation is real. Fix the retaliation and the
> rights start working on their own.

Supporting facts:
- Indiana is an at-will employment state. There is no general requirement that an
  employer show cause before terminating a frontline caregiver.
- IOSHA retaliation complaints must be filed with the Indiana Department of Labor within
  **30 calendar days** of the adverse action. Federal OSHA §11(c) is also 30 days.
  Sources: [Indiana DOL Whistleblower Protection Unit](https://www.in.gov/dol/iosha/whistleblower-protection-unit/),
  [OSHA fact sheet 3812](https://www.osha.gov/sites/default/files/publications/OSHA3812.pdf).
  **This 30-day window is the single most useful fact you can put in front of a worker.**
  Most people miss it because they spend the first month appealing internally.

## The policy ask (keep it to three, keep them concrete)

1. **A state minimum staffing standard** with public per-shift posting, since the federal
   floor is gone until at least 2034.
2. **Spend-down accountability**: require that Medicaid supplemental payments generated
   by a nursing home be spent on that nursing home's direct care, with public reporting
   by facility.
3. **Cause and anti-retaliation protection for frontline caregivers** who report unsafe
   conditions or refuse unsafe assignments — a documented, legitimate reason for
   termination, which preserves at-will employment generally while closing the retaliation
   loophole in healthcare settings.

Ask 3 is the one that is original to this project. Lead with 1 or 2 for broad audiences;
lead with 3 for worker audiences.

## Language rules

**Use:** short staffing · assignment · unsafe assignment · retaliation · documented ·
direct care · the floor · residents · frontline · accountability · where the money went

**Avoid:** "our most vulnerable" · "heartbreaking" · "warehouse" · "hellhole" ·
"abuse" as a casual descriptor · "criminal" applied to any named person · "always"/"every
facility" · anything implying a specific named home is dangerous

**Never publish:** resident names, initials, room numbers, photos, diagnoses, dates of
incidents tied to identifiable residents, coworker names, badge photos, schedules,
census sheets, assignment sheets, or anything photographed inside a facility.

## Answers to the four attacks you will get

**"You're just bitter / you got fired."**
> I'm one of thousands. Indiana is 50th in the country for staffing. That number isn't
> about me.

**"Nursing homes can't find anyone to hire."**
> Turnover isn't a labor shortage, it's a retention failure. Indiana homes got billions in
> supplemental Medicaid payments during the same years staffing fell to 50th. The money
> was there. It went to hospital buildings.

**"You're attacking caregivers."**
> The opposite. Every ask here protects caregivers. The people making staffing decisions
> aren't on the floor at 3am.

**"This is political."**
> Fiftieth out of fifty isn't a party position. Both parties have held this seat while it
> happened.
