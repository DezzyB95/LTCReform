# Visual Identity & Graphics Briefs

Buildable in Canva in an evening. No designer needed.

## Identity

The look should read **public-health notice**, not activist poster and not nonprofit
brochure. Calm, plain, slightly institutional. That's what makes it credible to a
skeptical CNA and screenshot-able for a reporter.

**Palette**
- Background: warm off-white `#F7F5F1`
- Text: near-black charcoal `#1C1C1A`
- Accent (numbers, rules, emphasis): deep slate blue `#2E4A62`
- Alert accent, used sparingly: muted clay `#A8503C`
- Never: bright red, gradients, neon, medical teal, drop shadows

**Type**
- Headline: a plain grotesque — Inter, Archivo, or Source Sans. Bold weight, tight
  leading, large.
- Body: same family, regular weight, generous line height.
- Numbers get to be enormous. `50th` should be the size of the card.
- No script fonts, no all-caps headlines, no letter-spacing effects.

**Layout**
- Wide margins. One idea per card. Nothing centered except a single-number card.
- Thin 1px accent rule under headlines.
- Source line, small, at the bottom of every factual card. Always.
- Logo/wordmark only on the last slide of a carousel — never on every slide. Repeated
  branding reads as marketing and depresses shares.

**Wordmark:** "Together Network" set plainly in the headline face, lowercase or sentence
case, no icon, no clasped hands, no heart, no stethoscope. The absence of a symbol is
itself a signal that this isn't a marketing operation.

## Template set (build these once, reuse forever)

### T1 — The Number Card
Full-bleed off-white. One giant figure, one line of context, one source line.
> **50th**
> Indiana's rank among all 50 states and D.C. for nursing home staffing hours per
> resident, adjusted for acuity.
> *Source: IndyStar analysis of federal data*

Variants: `$2.6 billion` · `88%` · `0.66` · `30 days` · `2034`

### T2 — The Math Card
Handwritten-notebook or plain-ledger feel. A visible subtraction.
> 480 minutes
> − report
> − charting
> − 2 meal passes
> − finding the lift
> ─────────────
> **under 20 minutes per resident**

### T3 — The Rights Card
Slate blue header bar, checklist body, disclaimer footer.
> **You're allowed to:**
> ✓ Talk to coworkers about staffing
> ✓ Talk about pay
> ✓ Talk about scheduling and safety
> ✓ Act together as a group
> *General information, not legal advice.*

### T4 — The Quote Card
Black background, white text, no attribution, no quotation marks styled as decoration.
Used only for the "They Told Us" series. Its severity comes from restraint.
> "You're a servant."

### T5 — The How-To Card
Numbered steps, clay accent on the number, contact info in a boxed footer.
Used for complaint filing, legislator contact, documentation.

### T6 — The Timeline Card
Horizontal or vertical dated sequence with a rule. Used for the staffing-rule repeal
story:
> May 2024 — federal minimum set
> Apr 2025 — struck down in court
> 2025 — Congress bars enforcement to 2034
> Feb 2026 — formally repealed
> **Indiana: never had one**

## Accessibility — not optional here

Your audience reads on cracked phone screens in dim break rooms at 3am.

- Minimum 16pt equivalent body text; bigger is better.
- Contrast at least 4.5:1. The palette above clears this; don't tint it.
- **Alt text on every image.** Write out the full text of the card.
- **Burned-in captions on every video.** Most viewing is muted, and a lot of it is in a
  room where sound isn't an option.
- Never put essential information only in an image — repeat key numbers in the caption so
  screen readers and search both catch them.

## Photography

Mostly don't. When you need an image:

- Use: empty hallway (not a real facility), scrubs in a laundry basket, a car dashboard
  before dawn, hands with a pen and a notebook, an empty break room table, a parking lot
  at 5:40am.
- Never: stock photos of smiling elderly people with caregivers. It's the visual language
  of the industry's own marketing and it will undercut everything the text says.
- Never: anything photographed inside an actual facility. No exceptions. See
  `09-LEGAL-SAFETY.md`.
